> “Next,” says Bertius, “we shall first recount the merits and benefits of the consequences that follow from our proposed conclusions. Second, we shall gather together the absurdities that necessarily accompany the opposing opinion.” {Pag. 63. l. 20.}

<!-- B002 -->
### Answer

<!-- B003 -->
Judging by the way Bertius proceeds here, he seems to have two eyes in his head: his right eye is good, and he turns it only on his own inventions; his left eye is bad, and he turns it only on his opponent. That is why he calls his opinion true and conducive to godliness: nothing flows from it but what is likewise true and worthy of belief, and conducive to godliness and good morals. {Pag 69. l. 9.} And that is why he calls our opinion false and harmful to all Christendom: nothing flows from it but what is false and harmful both to God’s honour and to good, upright morals. Besides, it gives rise to many presumptuous, endless, indeed foolish and ignorant questions. {pag. 83. l. 20.}

<!-- B004 -->
In short, Bertius holds that his view is the teaching of Jesus Christ and ours the teaching of Belial. He can see no evil at all in his own view and no good at all in ours. His, according to him, has always been believed and taught in God’s church; ours never, except by a few private individuals.

<!-- B005 -->
What then? Ought we not everywhere to introduce and adopt this Christian and salutary teaching of Bertius, and to root out and suppress our devilish and utterly harmful teaching? More than reasonable! And no doubt it would have come to that long ago had Bertius’s companions, in their native sweetness, not prevented it. They plead most urgently that we be allowed to carry on with our godless and damnable teaching, keep our pulpits, lead the poor people astray and blaspheme God daily. Their sole condition is that they, with their saving teaching, be tolerated alongside us. The common people are, after all, prophets to a man and have the spirit of discernment. {1. Cor. 14. 32. en cap. 12. 10.} <span class="marginal-translation" data-margin="M04">Marginal translation: 1. Cor. 14. 32. and chapter 12. 10.</span> Being so well equipped in such weighty matters to distinguish good from evil, truth from falsehood and what is profitable from what is harmful, they might thus hear everything and keep only what is most useful. {1. Thes. 5. 21.}

<!-- B006 -->
They do not doubt what would soon follow: seeing that Christ and Belial were now at one, people would trample all religion and godliness underfoot until at last, when we had become a Sodom, God would destroy us all from heaven with pitch, fire and sulphur and burn us in the abyss of hell, to eternal damnation and pain.[^burn]

<!-- B007 -->
But do not Bertius and his associates give a striking demonstration of their zeal for godliness, for God’s honour and for the salvation of many souls, when on the one hand they denounce our teaching on this point and its consequences as so harmful and damnable an opinion, yet on the other profess every wish to see us and our teaching openly tolerated in all lands, cities and churches?

<!-- B008 -->
What more can be said of the Papists, Arians, Socinians and other such unblessed sects than that, though they believe some truth as we do, on various points they put forward a teaching that is false and harmful to all Christendom? From it too flow things that are false and harmful both to God’s honour and to good, upright morals, just as Bertius says here of us. {Pag. 83. l. 20.}

<!-- B009 -->
Well then, why might we not work towards becoming, in time, a single sheepfold? {Joan. 10.16.} Why not let all sects take their turn in every church in this country, spreading their poison and sowing their evil seed?

<!-- B010 -->
They have also tried to lull everyone to sleep and make them believe that these recent differences in doctrine do not concern salvation. One opinion, they said, was somewhat sweeter and easier to grasp than the other and somewhat better suited to binding all the parts of the Christian religion together; but otherwise both opinions could be tolerated and even advocated without loss of salvation.

<!-- B011 -->
Now they sing quite the opposite tune. Those who are alive then will hear how things turn out. Had they spoken so plainly at the outset, they would have been smothered in the shell long ago. But now they see that people wish to be deceived, that their ears itch {2. Tim. 4.3.} and their stomachs turn at the old manna. {Nu. 11.4.6.} So they too seize their opportunity and help people get where they wish to be.

<!-- B012 -->
> But the Lord knows the thoughts of human beings, that they are vain. Blessed is the one whom you discipline, Lord, and teach through your law to have patience when things go badly, until the pit is ready for the ungodly. For the Lord will not reject his people or forsake his inheritance. For right must remain right, and all upright hearts will cleave to it. {Ps. 94.11.&c}

<!-- B013 -->
To come closer to the matter: note first that we acknowledge all the consequences Bertius draws here from his teaching to be good, profitable and edifying. Accordingly, we set these very things before our hearers every day, in all our sermons wherever it can suitably be done. The whole world is witness to this.

<!-- B014 -->
Second, note that in drawing these consequences from his teaching Bertius does not once prove that they do not follow from ours as well. Yet he ought to have done so if he wanted people to call his teaching alone good, true and profitable, and ours, by contrast, so bad, false and harmful. We say, then, that everything Bertius sets out here as a consequence of his teaching follows just as naturally from ours.

<!-- B015 -->
For we teach that God has determined to save people only through means, namely through faith, good works and perseverance in both. Likewise, he has determined to grant perseverance only through means, namely through keeping watch, praying, walking circumspectly, and so on.[^means]

<!-- B016 -->
So to draw the aforementioned [unreadable text] from his teaching, Bertius wishes to reason as follows, and must do so:

<!-- B017 -->
> Those who have been born again can fall away. Therefore those who have been born again must walk circumspectly, avoid sin, and so on.

<!-- B018 -->
In just the same way we too reason, and rightly so: God does not wish to give salvation to those who have been born again unless they persevere, nor does he wish to give them perseverance unless they walk circumspectly, grow in the practice of virtue and avoid sin, and so on. Therefore those who have been born again must persevere in order to be saved; and they must also walk circumspectly, grow in the practice of virtue and avoid sin, and so on, in order that they may persevere.

<!-- B019 -->
Third and last, note that although the consequences Bertius draws from his teaching are very profitable, it by no means follows that the teaching itself must therefore be true, good and profitable. {pag.69.l.11.}

<!-- B020 -->
For truth can indeed follow from a false proposition. All these consequences would likewise follow if someone said that the elect who are ordained to salvation could fall away.

<!-- B021 -->
We have now shown that these consequences follow from our teaching too. Yet Bertius will still consider our teaching false. Why then need we think any better of his?

<!-- B022 -->
If every teaching from which such consequences follow is good, then ours is good too. If ours is not good, then not every teaching from which these consequences are drawn is good. It follows that these consequences cannot show that Bertius’s teaching is good.

[^burn]: A proposed correction would read “banish us to the abyss of hell” rather than “burn us in the abyss of hell.”

[^means]: The parallel with salvation favours “only through means.” The abbreviation could also mean “to all,” specifying who receives perseverance rather than how it is given.
