## The sixth argument: the causes that prevent falling away

<!-- B002 -->
> Whoever can avoid falling away by striving for a good conscience, by diligence, prayer, watchfulness, careful and holy fear, faith, and close self-examination can also depart from righteousness by neglecting all these things. {pag.41.ll.6.}

<!-- B003 -->
> Now every righteous person can avoid falling away in this life by observing these things.

<!-- B004 -->
> Therefore every righteous person can also depart from righteousness in this life by neglecting them.

<!-- B005 -->
### Answer

<!-- B006 -->
I freely grant all this to Bertius. For I know of no teacher of the Reformed faith who holds predestination, and the perseverance that follows from it, to be so absolute or strict that the elect must persevere and be saved whatever they do. We have touched on this before. If anyone nevertheless holds that opinion, it is his own; the Church as a whole must not be charged with it. For my part, I do not hold it.

<!-- B007 -->
I say expressly that no one, whether you call him elect, regenerate, or anything else, will or can attain salvation unless he strives to keep a good conscience, watches, prays, fears, believes, is diligent, and does the other things of this kind that God has ordained as means to salvation.

<!-- B008 -->
Our whole dispute comes down to this: can a regenerate person leave off striving for a good conscience, watching, praying, and so on, and never take them up again? We deny it. This is what Bertius needed to prove here for his claim to have any bearing on the question.

<!-- B009 -->
As for the briefer demonstration he offers, I may let it pass without examining it, since it serves only to bolster his claim. {pa.39. en 40.} Besides, we have already answered some of the passages he cites, namely Matth.13.12. Philip.2.12. Roman.11.18. 1.Pet.1.5. {pag.39.li.36 en pag.40.li.32.§37.}

<!-- B010 -->
Some of the passages he cites do not even bear on his point, such as 1.Timoth.1.19. and cap.4.13.14. 2.Tim.1.6.14. {pag.39.li.30 35.37.38 en Pa40.li.4.} These teach how a preacher is to conduct himself in his office, not how a Christian is to conduct himself as a Christian.

<!-- B011 -->
Indeed, some passages speak directly against Bertius, such as 1.Joan.5.18. 1.Petr.1.5. {pag.39.li.34. pag.40.li.37.} We have shown this above, and anyone who simply reads the words can easily judge for himself.

<!-- B012 -->
In particular, Bertius also mentions here his fourth proposition, which he regards as beyond doubt. {pag.39.l.20.} There he says that perseverance in faith is a gift of God. {pag.9.lin.30.}

<!-- B013 -->
The proposition is indeed very much to the point: far from lending Bertius’s claim any support, it overthrows it entirely.

<!-- B014 -->
Read carefully what we said to explain that fourth proposition and compare it with the case before us. I have no doubt that you will find Bertius’s error so plain you could feel it with your hands. While boasting that both the ability to persevere and perseverance itself are gifts that God gives to whomever he pleases, Bertius is wrong nevertheless to ask whether a person can resist God’s working and forsake him. {pag.39 Lat.}

<!-- B015 -->
Yet it is no wonder that Bertius asks this. He holds that a person’s perseverance depends more on his own will than on God’s. {Pag.5.lin.6. en pag.13.l.4.21.26.}

<!-- B016 -->
If the saints will to persevere, says Bertius, they can; and it is God who gives them that ability. Since everyone who wills to persevere and can do so actually perseveres, actual perseverance must also be attributed to God, without whose grace we can do nothing at all. Yet on this account it must be attributed chiefly to the person himself. Not only can he, if he wishes, wickedly refuse to accept the grace offered him; he can also let the sufficient, indeed abundant, grace he has received bear no fruit in him, simply by choosing not to use it.

<!-- B017 -->
In short, if the person wills it, God wills it. If the person does not will it, well, so be it: God may take back his gifts and give them to another. Or perhaps these others were better disposed than the former. [The Dutch is unclear about how this last comparison follows from the transfer of the gifts.]

<!-- B018 -->
But if this is not a trick of Pelagian heresy, then, as we have shown above, Bertius himself has either misunderstood the teachings of Pelagius or at least failed to present them faithfully.

<!-- B019 -->
Our belief is that God gives his saints the will to persevere, so that they will to do so, and the strength to persevere, so that they can. Consequently, it is impossible for the saints to cease persevering. {Siet onse verclaringe over ’t vierde [onleesbaar] voorstel / en op andere plaetsen.} <span class="marginal-translation" data-margin="M10">Marginal translation: See our explanation of the fourth [unreadable] proposition, and elsewhere.</span>
