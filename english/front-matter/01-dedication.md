# To the well-born, noble, highly learned, very wise, discerning and honourable gentlemen, my lords the Nobility and Towns of the County of Zutphen

And in their name:

The noble, honourable, learned, very wise, discerning and respected gentlemen, my lords the Deputies of that same County.

And also:

The noble, honourable, highly learned, very wise, discerning and respected gentlemen, my lords the Magistrates of Zutphen, the principal town of the County, under whose authority I serve.

## My Lords,

It grieves all godly people deeply that so many polemical works appear every day. Those who profess in every way to uphold one and the same Reformed religion not only preach and dispute so vehemently against one another in person, but also attack and fight one another so publicly in print. For my part, I readily admit that there is more than enough reason for heartfelt grief. Yet the holy Apostle commands all faithful teachers to resist profane and idle talk {2. Tim. 2.16} and to keep watch {Acto. 20.28.} over themselves and the whole flock in which the Holy Spirit has made them overseers, to shepherd the congregation of God, which he acquired with his blood. I therefore think no one can rightly blame us if, to satisfy our conscience and fulfil our duty, we take up the defence of the truth with the very weapons its enemies use against it.

What then? People speak against the truth: may we not speak for it? People preach against the truth: may we not preach for it? People write against the truth: may we not write for it? We do no more than defend ourselves, as all divine and human laws allow. Countless books have been written against the Arians, Pelagians, Arbeisten, Jesuits and others like them. Has anyone ever taken offence at that?

It is with just such people that we now have to deal. They say, it is true, that they belong to the Reformed religion and intend to remain in it. But how do they show it? They teach that to understand Christ’s life, suffering, death, resurrection and ascension is not the eternal way to salvation, and that one is saved [wording unclear here] without knowledge of Christ’s person and merits. They teach that original sin is not, strictly speaking, sin and deserves no condemnation, but is a punishment for Adam’s sin; that we are not all born with original sin; and that not all human beings had the power to do good in Adam. They teach that the whole reason why one person believes and another does not lies not in God’s will alone but in both God’s will and human free will. They teach that it is not Christ’s righteousness that is imputed to us as our righteousness, but faith that is alive and active through love: it is precisely insofar as faith is of this kind that it is imputed to us as righteousness. They teach that the righteousness with which we shall stand before God is a righteousness within us, and that we are justified by the works of faith. They teach that true faith and true righteousness are not peculiar to the elect; indeed, even those who once truly possessed them can, and commonly do, abandon them entirely and finally.

These and other such teachings are now being put forward. The so-called Remonstrants or their followers champion them; we oppose them. But are these teachings of the Reformed religion that has been accepted, professed and preached for so many years in various kingdoms as well as here in this country? The whole world knows better.

Consider what these people have done. Not only have they sullied the doctrine of faith taught in our churches in the many ways I have in part recounted; they have also trampled underfoot the good and long-established order, power and authority of the consistories, classes and synods. They have all but deprived us of the means to repair the damage we have suffered or ever to regain our former well-being. Besides this, they exercise a freedom much like that of Julian’s day, the better to entice and stir up all libertines and lovers of novelty to lay further waste to our religion, indeed to the whole Christian religion. They have so incited the Christian civil authorities against us that in some places the authorities are already beginning to drive away faithful preachers and other good inhabitants, to forbid our people all gatherings for worship, public or private, and to show them a bitterness scarcely ever shown to Jews and Jesuits, even now. If anyone seriously considers all this together, I cannot believe they will regard these people as genuine lovers of the true Reformed religion.

Either our religion is the Reformed religion or it is not. If it is not, what Reformed religion was it that the illustrious, high and mighty Lords States General went to such incalculable expense and trouble to protect? For what religion did they appeal to so many kings and princes and proclaim so many days of prayer and fasting? The Papists, Mennonites, David-Jorists and others like them have certainly never been regarded as Reformed, and this Remonstrant faith first became known only a very few years ago. Knowing this well, the Remonstrants often say that we should not ask what has been held to be true until now, but what we should hold to be true from now on. If ours is the true Reformed religion, how can those who seek to suppress it be called its devoted supporters? Should we not rather call them its persecutors, since by their teaching and their other actions they set themselves so openly and bitterly against us? Why, then, should not all who love that religion from the heart be free to speak against such men and resist them? {Syr.4.33.} “Defend the truth to the death,” says the wise man, “and God the Lord will fight for you.”

My Lords, this duty falls not only to us who are teachers, but also to Your Lordships and to all other Christian civil authorities, high and low, who seek the good of God’s Church and our dear fatherland.

> The constitution of our commonwealth depends on godliness, says the praiseworthy Emperor Theodosius, and there is a close kinship and fellowship between the two. They hold together, and as one grows, so does the other. Thus true religion shines through the diligent pursuit of justice, and the commonwealth, helped by both, shines in turn. And if, says the same emperor, we can hold fast to the observance of true religion, we know that this will open the door to happiness and prosperity in whatever human affairs we may undertake.

All civil authorities, then, ought not only to practise religion themselves but also to bring their subjects to religion by fitting means and see that they observe it. But what religion is this to be? Certainly not one the authorities devise in their own hearts, {1.Reg.12.33} as Jeroboam is recorded to have done, but the religion God prescribes and commands in his holy Word. Once a country has acknowledged and accepted a religion as true and sound, it is not bound to persevere in it for ever. Yet neither should it lightly permit any breach of that religion without a prior lawful examination by those to whom God himself has entrusted the care of its doctrine. {Malac.2.7.} Otherwise we shall have something new each day, and by changing religion so lightly we shall in the end lose all religion. We have every reason to fear that this may yet happen here in these Netherlandish provinces if everyone is henceforth allowed such freedom to bring even into the pulpit whatever seems good to him and suits his fancy.

Your Lordships, like so many other Christian civil authorities in these lands, can readily see the truth of this. You have often shown your deep displeasure at the present disorder and have always earnestly desired and worked to ensure that we in this County and Town may live together in peace and unity, holding to the doctrine of truth we once clearly acknowledged and accepted. You still give what help you can, most willingly, in counsel and in deed, to find a happy way out of all these difficulties and at last restore God’s afflicted Church in our dear fatherland to its former happy state. In this you have rendered God a service most pleasing to him, and you still do so daily. You have thereby gained not only praise and honour among all right-minded lovers of God’s holy Word but also the special favour, heartfelt affection and prayers of all your godly subjects. Nor will the good God fail, in his own time, to reward such Christian zeal richly. For God is not unjust: he will not forget your work or the labour of love you have shown towards his name. {Ebr.6.10.} “Whoever honours me,” he says, “I too will honour.” {1.Sam.2.30}

For Christ Jesus’ sake, then, I beg Your Lordships always to continue promoting the good of God’s churches in every way, with the same courage you have shown until now. May we never, by turning even slightly to the right or to the left from the purity of the faith and the good, settled order without which God’s house can neither be governed nor stand, fall in our turn into the distress and misery in which, to our deep sorrow, we now see our neighbours.

I know well that this little book of mine, in which I defend nothing but the doctrine of truth, will have very many opponents and enemies and will make many for me as well. I therefore most humbly ask Your Lordships, who have shown me such kindness, honour and favour, to take both this little book and me under your protection and safekeeping.

## My Lords,

May Almighty God graciously grant Your Lordships a long, prosperous and blessed rule, for the glory of his holy name, the protection of our dear fatherland and the well-being of the true Reformed Church of his Son Jesus Christ. Amen.

From Zutphen, this 2 September, in the year 1613.

Your Lordships’  
always obedient servant,

Sebastianus Damman.
