# Preface to the Christian Reader

When word reached us here, beloved reader, that Bertius’s book on the falling away of the saints had appeared, I was as eager as many others to see and read it. So we wrote to our good friends in Holland and asked them to buy a copy and send it to us, since we thought it had been printed there. At first this was hard to arrange: it was said that copies were being held back and that most went first to those who favoured the new teachings. At long last, through the considerable efforts of one lover of the truth, I obtained a Latin copy. It was printed not in Holland but in Frankfurt, or so the imprint said; yet it bore no printer’s name. This struck me and others as strange. Stranger still, as soon as we began to read, we could tell from the differing page references that the book had already been printed in another format.

After I had read and reread the book and discussed it privately with a few people, I was asked what I thought of it. I said plainly that it had not satisfied me. The arguments Bertius used to support his view looked attractive, especially dressed up in such splendid Latin, but when brought into the light they did not amount to much. I was then asked to take up my pen and show, for the benefit of others, why I had said this. The same friends I mentioned earlier, the ones who were to buy the book for me, also wrote to ask me to do this. I undertook to do my best as far as my regular ministry allowed, though I thought, and said, that the task would better suit others who could give it more attention and do it better. I readily acknowledge my lack of learning and experience. Even so, I began to consider the matter seriously and devoted myself wholeheartedly to beginning the work in God’s name and carrying it through.

Not long afterwards, some people raised difficulties that made me seriously consider abandoning the work and sparing myself any further trouble. And that is just what I did. For a good year and a day I did not look at it again, not even once. I kept to my usual studies, although many different people urged and encouraged me, both by word of mouth and in writing, to continue. It is wrong to take counsel with flesh and blood in matters of God. But the Lord made provision for this. In May of last year someone sent me Bertius’s book in Dutch translation, with word that ordinary, unlearned people were reading it widely and that many praised it highly. There was good reason to fear that it might do great harm, especially if no one replied. For then it might seem that no one had been able to untangle his arguments, or perhaps even that no one had been willing to do so. That would have meant, as it were, abandoning so many godly souls.

This moved me so deeply—I speak the truth—that I resolved, whatever might come of it or become of me, to take up again with all my strength the work I had begun and then laid aside. I did so to defend God’s honour, to uphold the teaching of the truth, and to serve, comfort and edify all who love the truth. I thought that when a fire threatens everyone, each person not only may but must bring water. Though the help each can bring is not equal in extent or worth, no one can reasonably blame someone who does what he can. That is how I finally came to this work. I wanted you, Christian reader, to know this, so that you would not think I undertook it out of a high opinion of myself, or because I had nothing else to do, or because I enjoy getting into trouble.

As for the subject itself, I first set out the position of our churches and prove it from God’s Word, from reason, from the Netherlandish Confession and the Heidelberg Catechism accepted in the Netherlandish churches, from the agreement of the ancient teachers, and from the testimony of various leading councils and individual theologians of the Reformed churches in every land. Along the way I answer everything Bertius brings forward in his book against the proofs I have just given. Then I turn to the refutation of all the arguments Bertius uses to establish his claim. Since he divides his book into two questions, I too deal with the first question and then the second. I do not follow his footsteps at every point, but to my knowledge I leave nothing unanswered.

So that everyone can better judge which of us speaks and upholds the truth, Bertius or I, I have chosen not to quote him in full. Instead, I simply give the page and line in his book where he says what we dispute. Anyone who cares to consider the matter seriously can thus consult Bertius himself and at the same time hear him speak in my book. Where the people are only half present, as the saying goes, their words too are only half there. I feared that if I quoted Bertius in full at every point, my book would grow far too long. Yet if I tried to keep it short, someone might say I had left out something relevant, or at least had not adequately conveyed the true basis and force of his words. As it is, you can look them up yourself and compare what he says with what we say. On that score you can rest easy.

If I have gone wrong anywhere by saying too much or too little, or by not speaking clearly enough, I ask you to excuse me. For who can make sure that everyone is always equally pleased and understands equally well? I am human. If corrected, I will always gladly yield to reason and act accordingly as far as I can.

But you, beloved reader, do not turn away from the good you once learned. Stand firm, do not waver, and abound in the work of the Lord. See that no one carries you off through human wisdom and empty deception. Remember what the Apostle says {2. Tim. 4. 3.}:

> A time will come (and we are already living in it) when they will not tolerate sound teaching. Instead, as their ears itch, they will gather teachers to suit their own desires. They will turn their ears away from the truth and turn to fables.

Turn away, then, from such people. Fear God and seek to please him rather than to please others or satisfy the curiosity of your own heart, lest you begin to give way and in the end fall all at once. May the good God strengthen and preserve you in the true, saving faith in Jesus Christ, and have mercy on his afflicted Christendom. Amen.
