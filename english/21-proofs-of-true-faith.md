<!-- B001 -->
Let us therefore turn to Bertius’s argument.

<!-- B002 -->
> That the righteousness, he says, which the righteous person abandons is a true righteousness is proved by all the same arguments by which we proved the first conclusion, or part. From those many arguments we shall pick out a few, here and there. {Pag.60.li.5}

<!-- B003 -->
Answer. And we shall answer these selections briefly. But since Bertius appeals principally to the arguments he put forward in the first part, I ask the reader to look back at what we have answered to those same arguments, so as to understand better the matter at issue here and to be able to judge it more suitably.

<!-- B004 -->
## From the first proof.

<!-- B005 -->
> Righteousness, grace, God, the good salt, Jehovah, he who is in heaven, the Holy Spirit, and trust are true goods, not apparent or invented ones. Now the righteous person abandons all these together whenever he falls away. He therefore abandoned genuine goods. {Pag.60.l.9.}

<!-- B006 -->
Answer. It is one thing to speak of true goods, and another to speak of saving goods. Again, it is one thing to speak of true, saving goods, and another to speak of truly possessing, and consequently of truly abandoning, these true, saving goods. True faith and trust, true righteousness, holiness, and love are true goods. But all those who have and abandon faith, righteousness, and so forth neither have nor abandon true faith, righteousness, and so forth, as has been proved. All grace and gifts of the Holy Spirit are good; {1.Cor.12.9.} but not all are saving, or bring one to salvation, as is well known. God, Jehovah who is in heaven, is good—indeed, the highest good. Yet not all who depart or fall away from this good have truly possessed it, loved it, clung to it, or followed it. Truly possessing these true saving goods is a characteristic of those truly born again. And they never entirely abandon these true goods in which they have once truly shared, as we have shown very clearly and proved forcefully above. Read also our answer to Bertius’s first argument in the first part.

<!-- B007 -->
## From the second proof.

<!-- B008 -->
> The angels, Adam, and Eve have abandoned true goods. Faith, the way of righteousness, holy teaching, being a believing city, the fountain of living waters, the Lord by whom you were bought, and purity are genuine goods. {pag.60.l.16.}

<!-- B009 -->
Answer. I grant this; but they are not all saving goods for everyone. For many hypocrites profess the faith, know the way of righteousness and holy teaching, and know the Lord by whom they were bought and received into the number of his people. They are also outwardly cleansed—indeed, often adorned like beautiful painted tombs. They have also had believing ancestors, and so forth. Yet they are and remain hypocrites, estranged from eternal salvation. {Mat.23.27} But our question concerns those truly born again, who truly possess all the true and saving goods of the Holy Spirit and truly retain them to the end. Adam and Eve were not born again before the fall; neither were the angels who fell. These examples therefore do not pertain to our subject. And were not the angels cast out of heaven? Would they have us say, then, that even after we have entered heaven we must fear being cast out of it one day? See our answer to Bertius’s second argument in the first part.

<!-- B010 -->
## From the third proof.

<!-- B011 -->
> If someone abandons that in which he must remain to obtain eternal life, he falls from life and salvation into condemnation. Now one must remain steadfast in true righteousness, holiness, and faith; whoever abandons these virtues therefore falls. And {pag 61.li.2.} why? Because he abandons true goods. Hence, first, his former righteousness was genuine. Secondly, whoever abandons this deprives himself of true faith. Thirdly, he was a true vine-branch and was incorporated into Christ. Fourthly, he has truly tasted the heavenly gift, has shared in the Holy Spirit, and has tasted the powers of the life to come. Let one add here the passages Colos.1.22, 1.Timoth.2.15. But if things go otherwise, then, as the common saying goes, the little song is sung: 2.Petr.2.10. Apoc.3.11.

<!-- B012 -->
Answer. That someone who abandons the true righteousness, holiness, and faith in which he must remain to obtain eternal life then falls from eternal life and salvation into condemnation is beyond all dispute. But can anyone abandon this true righteousness, holiness, and faith? Bertius does not prove that with his third argument, as may readily be gathered from the reply we gave to it. Consequently, everything Bertius says here is of no value. As for the words of the apostle Paul in Collos.1.22. and 1.Tim.2.15., they too are irrelevant—unless Bertius wishes to fish something to his advantage out of the word “remain”. For surely no one can remain in faith, love, and sanctification unless he is already in them. And if remaining in faith means they will be saved, then that faith must be true, since without true faith no one can be saved. And if those same people do not remain in faith, they are condemned because they have abandoned the true faith in which they were.⁽ᵇ⁾ But this too does not touch our question, as has just been pointed out. For one would have to prove that someone who is founded in true faith can fall away from that faith. It is clear that those who remain in true faith will be saved, and that if they abandon true faith they will be condemned. But it is not clear that anyone has abandoned true faith, or can abandon it: that is the whole matter of our dispute. As for the saying that whoever abandons the faith by remaining in which he could have been saved must surely be abandoning a true faith, this is a great misunderstanding. It arises from wanting to judge the truth of someone’s faith strictly, as though by an infallible judgment, whereas one ought only to think and judge the best of it in charity, as Bertius himself also acknowledges elsewhere. {Pa.52.l.39.} {Pag.53.l.25} For look: in the words cited above the apostle Paul testifies that the Colossians were founded in true faith, saying:

<!-- B013 -->
> If you remain founded in faith. {Coloss.1.23}

<!-- B014 -->
By faith he undoubtedly means true faith, without which no one can be saved. No one can remain founded unless he is already founded. Therefore the Colossians were founded in true saving faith, says Paul. But did Paul know this with such certainty that no one could doubt it? Oh no! For Paul did not know the hidden recesses of the heart; rather, he hoped this because they testified thus about themselves. Therefore he says, “If you remain founded in faith”; that is, if you are founded in faith, as you say, and as you present yourselves, and as I too trust, and if you then remain founded in that same faith, you will be saved. But if the Colossians had now fallen away, would they then have fallen away from true faith alone? That cannot by any means be concluded. This, however, can: that the Colossians then testified otherwise about themselves in words and outward appearance, and people had consequently formed, in charity, an opinion of them and a trust in them that did not correspond to their true condition. Thus they will now be condemned, not because they have abandoned true faith, but because they never had true faith; that is, not because they failed to remain founded in true faith, but because they were never founded in true faith. For temporary believers have no root in themselves, and they are founded on sand, not on Christ, the immovable rock. {Lucæ 8.13.} {Mat.7.24 26}

<!-- B015 -->
> The woman will be saved in childbearing, says the same apostle, if she remains in faith, love, and sanctification. {1.Tim.2.15.}

<!-- B016 -->
But what if she had a false faith? Or an unstable, wavering faith? Or an empty delusion which she called faith? And if her love and sanctification were a feigned work, would she nevertheless be saved if she remained in that faith and love? By no means; but if she remained in true faith and unfeigned love. Yet do all women have such true faith and love? Certainly not. Nevertheless, it is also reasonable to give credence to everyone who testifies of himself that he has true faith and to hope that his testimony is true. {pag.61.l.17.} Therefore understand the apostle thus: the women who are in true faith, love, and sanctification will be saved if they remain in that same faith. But if they do not remain, what then? They will not be saved—not because they have abandoned true faith, but because they have not had true faith. This they show by turning back; for had they been of us, they would have remained with us. And if they had ever rightly known and seen Christ and been in him, they would have remained in him and would not have given themselves over to sin again. {1. Joan.2.19 En cap.3.6.} <span class="marginal-translation" data-margin="M14">Marginal translation: 1. John 2.19 and chapter 3.6.</span> As for the further words of the apostle Peter which Bertius cites here, they have already been answered several times: {2 Petr. 2.20} {pag 61.l.24} there is no need to repeat that here. From the admonition which the Lord Christ addresses to the bishop of the congregation in Philadelphia, Bertius constructs the following syllogism. {Apoc. 3.11} {Pag. 61. l. 3}

<!-- B017 -->
> Whoever can have his crown taken from him must hold on to what he has.
> You, O bishop, can have your crown taken from you.
> Therefore you must hold on to what you have. {Pag. 119. l. 8}

<!-- B018 -->
But what does Bertius mean by the words “your crown”? That incorruptible, undefiled, unfading inheritance which is kept in heaven for you? {1. Petr. 1.4.} If it is kept in heaven by this bishop⁽ᵈ⁾, it could not be taken from him. Or does he mean the glory promised to all faithful bishops and teachers above other people? {Dan. 12.3.} {1. Cor. 15.41} I think this is most likely what Christ meant. But that does not touch our disagreement. For even if someone behaved badly and unfaithfully in his preaching office, so that he either became a stench even to other people, or at least was not pleasing to his Lord, and would therefore certainly come to miss this crown, he could nevertheless, if he truly repented of his misconduct, certainly still obtain eternal salvation. And that is precisely our question: whether those who are born again can fall away from eternal salvation. Therefore I grant Bertius his aforesaid syllogism in its entirety, and acknowledge that this bishop could, through his unfaithfulness, lose the crown which he would receive as a bishop if he remained faithful. Christ addresses this bishop as a bishop, not as someone born again; he admonishes him to perform his episcopal office, not the duty he shared with all born-again Christians. The promise and threat added here must therefore also be taken and understood with that restriction or in that respect.

<!-- B020 -->

<!-- B021 -->
### Answer.

<!-- B022 -->
See and read our answer to Bertius’s fourth argument in the first part.

<!-- B023 -->
## From the fifth proof.

<!-- B024 -->
> Faith and a good conscience are not apparent goods, but genuine ones. To have a good which God afterwards takes away through his righteous judgment is surely not to have a feigned good. {pag. 62. li. 5.} The word of the kingdom is not an invented good. Furthermore, sins against conscience, carelessness, slackness, contempt for opportunities offered, terror of persecutions, love of pleasure and the enticements of the flesh, and so forth, do not draw us away from a semblance and appearance of good, but from the true good itself.

<!-- B025 -->
### Answer.

<!-- B026 -->
Bertius intends to prove that the faith, righteousness, and holiness which are abandoned are true and saving faith, true righteousness, and holiness, and that those who abandon them truly possessed them. Does that, then, not fit this subject nicely? Is the word of the kingdom a true faith? And is a good conscience always a consequence or fruit of true faith? {Rom 2.15.} To profess the faith, to have much knowledge and other goods or gifts of the Holy Spirit—is that to have saving faith? Bertius himself knows better. As for the other things which Bertius lists here and says draw us away from the true good, it is as he says: I acknowledge that. For not only God’s word but also daily experience testifies to this most abundantly. But what follows from it? Do the aforesaid things draw us away from the true saving faith which we have once received? From that true righteousness and holiness in which we have genuinely come to share? And do they draw us away from this righteousness, holiness, and faith in such a way that we entirely lose these true goods and always remain without them? Bertius does not prove this in his fifth argument, as may readily be understood from our answer to that same argument. Consequently, even though many people are drawn away from a true good which they have already obtained, and even though many more are drawn away from a true good which they have not obtained, being prevented by the aforesaid things from ever obtaining it, nevertheless, as long as it is not shown that every true good is a true faith or a saving good, or that any saving good is entirely taken away from someone so that he does not afterwards regain it, Bertius has accomplished nothing by all his saying, either to his advantage or to our detriment.

<!-- B027 -->
## From the sixth proof.

<!-- B028 -->
> The pursuit of a good conscience, reflection on God’s benefits, prayers, watchings, anxious and holy fear, and close self-examination do not maintain and nourish an invented good in us, but a genuine good. {pag.62.l.19.}

<!-- B029 -->
### Answer.

<!-- B030 -->
Indeed, sometimes a genuine evil. For by these means many are advanced and strengthened in a false faith which they have, as is well known. But we are not dealing here with some good which is maintained and nourished in us, but with a good which, after obtaining it, we subsequently lose again wholly and entirely. See also, in the first part, Bertius’s sixth argument, with our reply.

<!-- B031 -->
## From the seventh proof.

<!-- B032 -->
> He whose sins are forgiven is truly righteous; he from whom the evil spirit has once departed has truly been cleansed, and so forth. {Ibid. l.25.}

<!-- B033 -->
### Answer.

<!-- B034 -->
What is to be said about this has been said in the answer to Bertius’s last proof in the first part. Read it through carefully, and I hope you will be well satisfied as far as this matter is concerned.

<!-- B035 -->
## A special proof.

<!-- B036 -->
> Let there be added here in particular the express testimony of the holy apostle Peter, in his second epistle, chapter 2, verse 18. {Pag.63.l.2.}

<!-- B037 -->
### Answer.

<!-- B038 -->
It is one thing to have escaped those who live in error, and another to have escaped error itself. Many indeed leave the Jews, the heathens, and the Papists, yet in their hearts they remain attached to Judaism, heathenism, and Popery. Likewise, although Lot’s wife had bodily gone out of Sodom, and the Israelites out of Egypt, in thought and desire they still remained in Sodom and in Egypt.⁽ᵍ⁾ {Gen.19.26.} {Luc.17.32.} {Exod.16.3.} See in the first part the answer to Bertius’s fifth argument.

<!-- B039 -->
So much, then, for this brief account of the arguments by which Bertius seeks to prove this last conclusion of his. They are all so lame, feeble and irrelevant, that it seems to me that Bertius must have had far too high an opinion of himself, and far too low an opinion of others, if he thought he would thus make intelligent people believe that his opinion on this point agreed with God’s holy word and with good, sound reason.


## Reading notes

⁽ᵇ⁾ Consequence. The conditional and its explanation are clear; the preceding words could mean “those following.” The smooth inferential reading would depend on disputed *dien volgende*. No connecting phrase has been supplied as a settled correction.

⁽ᵈ⁾ The crown. The reading edition follows *door*, “by this bishop.” The immediately preceding “kept in heaven for you” also gives the rival “for this bishop” some support; the difference is between custodian and beneficiary.

⁽ᵍ⁾ Lot and Israel. The outward departure but inward attachment of the preceding example favours “in thought and desire they still remained” (*met hare*). The supplied *niet hare* contains a negative whose attachment cannot be established; it could reverse the point. The positive reading is editorial.
