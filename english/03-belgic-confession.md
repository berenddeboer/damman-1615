<a id="source-block-000"></a>

Let us begin with the Confession. First consider these words from Article 24:

<a id="source-block-001"></a>

> It is therefore impossible for this holy faith to be idle in a person; for we are not speaking of an empty faith, but of such a faith as Scripture calls a faith active through love, which moves a person to practise the works that God has commanded in his Word. {1.} {Joan. 15.5. 1. Timo 1.5. Galat. 5.6. Tit. 3.8.}

<a id="source-block-002"></a>

Observe: 1. A faith that is never idle in a person, but always moves him to good works—indeed, is itself active through love—certainly cannot be cast away. For Matth. 13.12. says, “To him who has, it shall be given.” And Joan. 15.2. says, “My Father cleanses everyone who bears fruit, so that he may bear more fruit.”

2. But the clearest way to see what the Confession means is to compare the words just quoted with those immediately before them. The preceding words describe two kinds of fruit that true faith bears. The first is regeneration, through which a person is brought to new life and freed from slavery to sin. {1 Petr. 1.23. Actor 15.9. Joa 8.34.36} The other is this: in those who have once been made alive, faith continues to work within them, moving them to do good and giving them a lasting concern to do so. There we certainly see a clear distinction between the beginning and the completion of our sanctification, and at the same time clear proof that both of these arise through faith. Thus a faith that merely regenerates a person (if this could indeed happen) cannot be regarded as genuine faith. Rather, this must necessarily be added: that it brings regeneration to completion—that is, that it remains in the regenerate to the end.

For someone might say that faith works in a person only so long as it is in him, and that, since it can be cast out by the person, it can then also cease to work. I answer that it is indeed true that faith works in a person no longer than it is in him. But the Confession denies that a person can cast faith out and that faith can therefore cease to work. For it is impossible for holy faith to be idle so long as it is in a person; that is, it is necessary that faith should be active through love and move the person to good works so long as it is in him. This teaches us that it is impossible for holy faith to die away within a person. For a faith without works is dead; but holy faith is not without works. {Iacob. 2.20} Indeed, it is impossible for it to be without works. Consequently, it is also impossible for it to be dead so long as it is in a person, and therefore it is impossible for it to die away within a person. But when the person dies, only then do faith and hope also die; true love alone still remains in the person for eternity, as Saint Paul teaches us, 1. Corinth. 13 8.

<a id="source-block-003"></a>

> Article 29. As for those who are OF THE CHURCH, one can recognize them by the marks of Christians, namely faith: {2.} {Ephes. 1.13. Ioan. 17.20.} when they have received the only Saviour, Jesus Christ, they flee sin and pursue righteousness, loving God and their neighbours, turning aside neither to the right nor to the left, and crucifying their flesh with its works. {1. Ioan. 3.9. en cap. 4.2. Rom. 6.2. Gal 5.17. Rom. 7.6.} It must nevertheless be understood that there is still great weakness in them, but they fight against it through the Spirit ALL the days of their lives, constantly taking refuge in the blood, death, suffering, and obedience of the Lord Jesus Christ, in whom they have forgiveness of their sins through faith in him. {Coloss. 1.14.}

<a id="source-block-004"></a>

Observe here: 1. If the struggle between Spirit and flesh continues in people for as long as they live, then the Spirit must also remain in them for as long as they live. In those who are OF THE CHURCH—that is, in all the regenerate—there is a struggle between Spirit and flesh ALL the days of their lives, that is, so long as they live. The conclusion follows. As for those whom we see every day drawing back and willingly committing sin without struggle or remorse, and who in the end perish on that account, we say that they have indeed been IN THE CHURCH, but not OF THE CHURCH. What St John says also bears on this: {1. Joan. 2.19}

<a id="source-block-005"></a>

> They have gone out from us, but they were not of us; for had they been of us, they would have remained with us. But this happened to show them for what they were, so that it might be clear that not all of them are of us.

<a id="source-block-006"></a>

2. If only those are OF THE CHURCH who believe in Christ, flee sin, pursue righteousness, love God and their neighbours, do not turn aside, crucify their flesh with its works, fight against sin, and, when they fall, do not stay down but take refuge in Christ’s blood, what becomes of that view of predestination which considers it entirely in isolation, without any means by which it takes effect? And what becomes of the perseverance founded on it? What a stink is raised over this, and how foully the churches in this land are smeared on its account! These words of our common Confession are surely clear and indisputable. That is why Bertius lets this pass without comment, as he does the preceding passage.

<a id="source-block-007"></a>

> Article 34. Baptism testifies to us that he will be our God forever and a gracious Father to us. {3.}

<a id="source-block-008"></a>

“Us” means not only those who are being baptized but also those of us who have been baptized and now believe. Bertius too understands these words in this way, for he writes: {Pag. 82. l. 16}

<a id="source-block-009"></a>

> The sacraments in the New Testament seal to believers not a temporal but an eternal grace.

<a id="source-block-010"></a>

Observe: how can God be the eternal God and Father of believers if they do not remain his children eternally?

<a id="source-block-011"></a>

### Bertius’s Objection.

<a id="source-block-012"></a>

> The Confession sets out here only that part of the covenant which concerns God. {pag 120. lin. 4.}

<a id="source-block-013"></a>

### Answer.

<a id="source-block-014"></a>

That is true, and it is enough for our purpose, since this is a covenant of grace and not of works, as we have already discussed at greater length in explaining the words of Jerem. 31.33. Consequently it is unnecessary for Bertius to ask whether we cannot neglect our duty, etc. {Ibid. li. 6.} For our duty is only to believe and to persevere in faith, whereas God’s duty is to give us faith and perseverance in faith, as he has promised and as we have said. When God fulfils his duty, we cannot neglect ours.
