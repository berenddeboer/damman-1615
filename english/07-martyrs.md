<a id="s07-b000"></a>

It is true that not all the martyrs were condemned or killed specifically for this point. {Pa.46. li.8.} But why was that? Because the Antichrist cared more for his own honour and for all human inventions and superstitions—indeed, devilish ones—than for the honour and teaching of God. Yet it is also certain that those French, English, and Netherlandish confessions all belong not only to the teachers but to the congregations as a whole. The martyrs, then, died holding fast not only to what they confessed before their judges and persecutors but also to the rest of what they believed, about which they were not questioned. Had anyone wished to examine them on those beliefs, they would undoubtedly have confessed them just as faithfully and steadfastly as the rest. I shall prove this with one or two examples.

<a id="s07-b001"></a>

In 1414 the renowned Johannes Hus was burned alive at Constance for what his tonsured fellows called heresy. They laid before him thirty-nine articles setting out this supposed heresy of his. Among them were the following:

<a id="s07-b002"></a>

> No member or part of the true Church can fall away permanently. {Art.3.}

<a id="s07-b003"></a>

Also:

<a id="s07-b004"></a>

> Some appear to be in the true Church but are not in it. Others are thought to be outside the Church because they live wickedly. Yet in view of predestination, or by reason of it, they remain members of the Church. {Art.4.}

<a id="s07-b005"></a>

Also:

<a id="s07-b006"></a>

> Predestination makes someone a member of the universal Church. Judas was chosen by Christ and received temporary gifts for carrying out his apostleship. Although people regarded him as a true disciple of Christ, he was nevertheless not a true disciple, but a wolf in sheep’s clothing. {Art.5. en 7.} <span class="marginal-translation" id="s07-m04">[Marginal translation: Articles 5 and 7.]</span>

<a id="s07-b007"></a>

Is this not stated clearly enough?

<a id="s07-b008"></a>

Likewise, in 1558 Villegagnon had three godly men executed in Brazil for what he called their heresy. Among other things, they said this in their confession:

<a id="s07-b009"></a>

> A person who is predestined to eternal life, although he sins through human weakness, nevertheless cannot fall into impenitence. On this point Saint John says that he does not sin, because election remains in him. {Art.10.}

<a id="s07-b010"></a>

And they died holding to that confession. I could name more such examples from books of martyrs and other histories. But do the examples I have cited not show clearly enough that the very teaching Bertius attacks and persecutes with his pen was already attacked and persecuted with fire and sword by the Whore of Babylon 200 years ago?

Praise, then, to the good God! He has preserved his Church so powerfully and wonderfully that all the tyrants’ cruelty has not uprooted it. More than that, he has blessed it so gloriously that, like a palm tree, it has spread its branches far and wide even beneath its burden and has filled so many kingdoms, countries, and cities. {Ps.80 9. &c} {Sanguis Martyrum est semen Ecclesiæ} <span class="marginal-translation" id="s07-m07">[Marginal translation: The blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church.]</span> That same God still lives. I do not doubt that, for the honour of his name, he will continue to shelter and protect that same Church of his with his mighty arm. {Zach.12.2.} Unless they repent, he will put to shame all who persecute it, fight against it, disturb it, grieve it, and cause it to stumble.

<a id="s07-b011"></a>

> Lord God of hosts, look upon this and speak your rebuke, so that the burning and tearing may come to an end. Comfort us and let your face shine, and we shall be healed. {Ps.80.17.19}

<a id="s07-b012"></a>

Let this much now be said [a word is unclear here] concerning the truth that Bertius contradicts in his book. What we believe and teach about the perseverance of the truly regenerate children of God was not fished out of some stinking pool of human opinions. It was drawn from the clear, unadulterated fountain of the prophets and apostles, who spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit. {2.Pet. 1. 21} This teaching did not first come to light a few years ago. For as long as Christ’s Church has been on earth, it has always been believed and always taught, though it has been stated more clearly at some times than at others, as circumstances allowed.

Nor was it, like many other things, taught long ago by some or even by many, only to be rejected by those whom God afterwards raised up expressly to cleanse the Christian religion of all scum and filth. Those very people acknowledged and accepted it as pure truth and stoutly defended it. Unlike some other teachings, it has been accepted not only in other kingdoms but here too in our own dear fatherland; and not here alone, but in many other countries and kingdoms as well. Almost everywhere it has been sealed and confirmed with the blood of countless martyrs.

And that is to say nothing of the many thousands from Brabant, Flanders, Artois, and Hainaut who left their native land, possessions, stations in life, and friends for that same truth. To this day we see them going about their lives with joy, holding fast to that truth, much to the vexation of Bertius and his associates. Yet in their pious, God-fearing souls they grieve deeply over the bitter disputes that have lately arisen. Whoever denies all this is shameless; whoever contradicts it is foolish; whoever makes light of it is unchristian; whoever is unmoved by it is unfeeling; whoever mocks it is openly godless.
